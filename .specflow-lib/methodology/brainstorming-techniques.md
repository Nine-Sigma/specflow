# Brainstorming Techniques

60 techniques across 10 categories for structured ideation.

## Anti-Bias Protocol

**LLM Semantic Drift Warning:** When using brainstorming techniques with AI:
- Techniques should push BEYOND default LLM responses
- If AI gravitates toward "sensible" answers, the technique isn't working
- Force uncomfortable, unexpected, counterintuitive ideas
- Explicitly request "weird" or "bad" ideas to break semantic drift

## Categories

### Collaborative (5 techniques)

#### Yes And Building

Build momentum through positive additions where each idea becomes a launching pad.

**How to use:**
- Use prompts like "Yes and we could also..."
- Use "Building on that idea..." to continue
- Create energetic collaborative flow that builds upon previous contributions

#### Brain Writing Round Robin

Silent idea generation followed by building on others' written concepts - gives quieter voices equal contribution while maintaining documentation.

**How to use:**
- Write ideas silently
- Pass ideas to next person
- Build on received concepts
- Continue the sequence of writing, passing, and building

#### Random Stimulation

Use random words/images as creative catalysts to force unexpected connections - breaks through mental blocks with serendipitous inspiration.

**How to use:**
- Ask how random elements relate to the problem
- Explore what connections exist
- Force relationships between unrelated items

#### Role Playing

Generate solutions from multiple stakeholder perspectives to build empathy while ensuring comprehensive consideration.

**How to use:**
- Embody different roles
- Ask what each stakeholder wants
- Explore how they'd approach problems
- Identify what matters most to them

#### Ideation Relay Race

Rapid-fire idea building under time pressure creates urgency and breakthroughs - prevents overthinking through creative momentum.

**How to use:**
- Structure with 30-second additions
- Build quickly on previous ideas
- Pass fast to maintain momentum

### Creative (11 techniques)

#### What If Scenarios

Explore radical possibilities by questioning all constraints and assumptions - perfect for breaking through stuck thinking.

**How to use:**
- Ask "What if we had unlimited resources?"
- Ask "What if the opposite were true?"
- Ask "What if this problem didn't exist?"

#### Analogical Thinking

Find creative solutions by drawing parallels to other domains - transfer successful patterns from elsewhere.

**How to use:**
- Ask "This is like what?"
- Ask "How is this similar to...?"
- Ask "What other examples come to mind?"
- Connect to existing solutions in other domains

#### Reversal Inversion

Deliberately flip problems upside down to reveal hidden assumptions and fresh angles - great when conventional approaches fail.

**How to use:**
- Ask "What if we did the opposite?"
- Ask "How could we make this worse?"
- Ask "What's the reverse approach?"

#### First Principles Thinking

Strip away assumptions to rebuild from fundamental truths - essential for breakthrough innovation.

**How to use:**
- Ask "What do we know for certain?"
- Ask "What are the fundamental truths?"
- Ask "If we started from scratch?"

#### Forced Relationships

Connect unrelated concepts to spark innovative bridges through creative collision.

**How to use:**
- Take two unrelated things
- Find connections between them
- Identify bridges
- Explore how they could work together

#### Time Shifting

Explore solutions across different time periods to reveal constraints and opportunities.

**How to use:**
- Ask "How would this work in the past?"
- Ask "What about 100 years from now?"
- Ask "What different era constraints apply?"
- Ask "What time-based solutions apply?"

#### Metaphor Mapping

Use extended metaphors as thinking tools to explore problems from new angles - transforms abstract challenges into tangible narratives.

**How to use:**
- State "This problem is like [metaphor]"
- Extend the metaphor fully
- Map elements from metaphor to problem
- Discover insights from the mapping

#### Cross-Pollination

Transfer solutions from completely different industries or domains to spark breakthrough innovations.

**How to use:**
- Ask how industry X would solve this
- Ask what patterns work in field Y
- Ask how to adapt solutions from domain Z

#### Concept Blending

Merge two or more existing concepts to create entirely new categories - goes beyond simple combination to genuine innovation.

**How to use:**
- Ask what emerges when concepts merge
- Ask what new category is created
- Explore how the blend transcends original ideas

#### Reverse Brainstorming

Generate problems instead of solutions to identify hidden opportunities and unexpected pathways.

**How to use:**
- Ask "What could go wrong?"
- Ask "How could we make this fail?"
- Ask "What problems could we create?"
- Reverse the insights to find solutions

#### Sensory Exploration

Engage all five senses to discover multi-dimensional solution spaces beyond purely analytical thinking.

**How to use:**
- Ask what the idea feels like
- Ask what it smells or tastes like
- Ask what it sounds like
- Explore how different senses engage with the problem

### Deep (8 techniques)

#### Five Whys

Drill down through layers of causation to uncover root causes - essential for solving problems at source rather than symptoms.

**How to use:**
- Ask "Why did this happen?"
- Ask "Why?" again to the answer
- Repeat until reaching fundamental drivers
- Usually takes 5 iterations to reach ultimate causes

#### Morphological Analysis

Systematically explore all possible parameter combinations for complex systems requiring comprehensive solution mapping.

**How to use:**
- Identify key parameters
- List options for each parameter
- Try different combinations systematically
- Identify patterns that emerge

#### Provocation Technique

Use deliberately provocative statements to extract useful ideas from seemingly absurd starting points - catalyzes breakthrough thinking.

**How to use:**
- Ask "What if [provocative statement]?"
- Ask "How could this be useful?"
- Ask "What idea does this trigger?"
- Extract the principle from the provocation

#### Assumption Reversal

Challenge and flip core assumptions to rebuild from new foundations - essential for paradigm shifts.

**How to use:**
- Ask "What assumptions are we making?"
- Ask "What if the opposite were true?"
- Challenge each assumption explicitly
- Rebuild from new assumptions

#### Question Storming

Generate questions before seeking answers to properly define problem space - ensures solving the right problem.

**How to use:**
- Ask only questions, no answers yet
- Focus on what we don't know
- Identify what we should be asking
- Let questions reveal the real problem

#### Constraint Mapping

Identify and visualize all constraints to find promising pathways around or through limitations.

**How to use:**
- Ask what all constraints exist
- Identify which are real vs imagined
- Explore how to work around barriers
- Consider how to eliminate constraints

#### Failure Analysis

Study successful failures to extract valuable insights and avoid common pitfalls - learns from what didn't work.

**How to use:**
- Ask what went wrong
- Ask why it failed
- Ask what lessons emerged
- Apply failure wisdom to current challenge

#### Emergent Thinking

Allow solutions to emerge organically without forcing linear progression - embraces complexity and natural development.

**How to use:**
- Ask what patterns emerge
- Ask what wants to happen naturally
- Ask what's trying to emerge from the system
- Let the solution develop organically

### Structured (7 techniques)

#### SCAMPER Method

Systematic creativity through seven lenses for methodical product improvement and innovation.

**How to use:**
- **S**ubstitute: What could you substitute?
- **C**ombine: What could you combine?
- **A**dapt: How could you adapt?
- **M**odify: What could you modify?
- **P**ut to other uses: What other uses?
- **E**liminate: What could you eliminate?
- **R**everse: What could you reverse?

#### Six Thinking Hats

Explore problems through six distinct perspectives without conflict - ensures comprehensive analysis from all angles.

**How to use:**
- **White Hat:** Focus on facts only
- **Red Hat:** Express emotions and intuition
- **Yellow Hat:** Identify benefits
- **Black Hat:** Consider risks
- **Green Hat:** Generate creative ideas
- **Blue Hat:** Manage the process

#### Mind Mapping

Visually branch ideas from central concept to discover connections and expand thinking - perfect for organizing complex thoughts.

**How to use:**
- Put main idea in center
- Branch concepts outward
- Add sub-branches for details
- See the big picture emerge

#### Resource Constraints

Generate innovative solutions by imposing extreme limitations - forces essential priorities and creative efficiency.

**How to use:**
- Ask "What if you had only $1?"
- Ask "What if you had no technology?"
- Ask "What if you had one hour to solve?"
- Ask "What if you had minimal resources only?"

#### Decision Tree Mapping

Map out all possible decision paths and outcomes to reveal hidden opportunities and risks - visualizes complex choice architectures.

**How to use:**
- Identify all possible paths
- Mark decision points
- Explore where different choices lead
- Find the optimal path

#### Solution Matrix

Create systematic grid of problem variables and solution approaches to find optimal combinations and discover gaps.

**How to use:**
- Identify key variables
- List solution approaches
- Test combinations in a grid
- Identify most effective pairings

#### Trait Transfer

Borrow attributes from successful solutions in unrelated domains to enhance approach - systematically adapts winning characteristics.

**How to use:**
- Ask what traits make success X work
- Ask how to transfer these traits
- Ask what they'd look like in your context
